Abstract

If you’ve maintained a coding-agent harness for any length of time—whether it’s orchestrating linting, testing, deployment, or some combination—you know the scaffolding just keeps piling up. And here’s the question that actually haunts you: which parts can I rip out or restructure without silently breaking the autonomy guarantees the whole thing was built to enforce?

This post recasts that question in the language of symplectic geometry. The idea: model the harness as a phase space with a Hamiltonian that encodes your autonomy constraints. Then Noether’s theorem hands you a complete set of conservation laws that any autonomy-preserving refactor has to respect. Symplectic reduction, in turn, gives you a canonical, provably safe procedure for stripping redundant scaffolding while keeping the orbit type of the autonomy dynamics intact.

The practical payoff? A CI-auditable checklist. Compute the moment map for each declared symmetry, verify non-degeneracy of the constraint submanifold, and confirm the reduced phase space carries the same orbit structure as the original. There’s a ten-line worked example and a concrete integration recipe for GitHub Actions and GitLab CI in §6.

This builds on the rate-distortion cost floor from The Autonomy Channel and the categorical preservation criterion in Functorial Autonomy.

Series context. This is the conservation-law entry in a progressive series on building a fully autonomous coding-agent harness from the ground up. See the series navigation at the end.

Key Takeaways

  • The CI audit is one command. Compute the Noether current \(J_i = \langle\mu, \xi_i\rangle\) for each declared symmetry \(\xi_i\) of your harness manifest. If \(\dot{J}_i = 0\) under the refactored dynamics, you’re good—the refactor is autonomy-preserving.
  • Non-degeneracy is the safety gate. The constraint submanifold \(\mathcal{C} = \{p_i = 0\}\) (the zero-momentum / “stage-idle” configurations) has to be a symplectic leaf. In CI, that’s just a rank test on the restriction of \(\omega\) to \(\mathcal{C}\).
  • Two-stage reduction protocol. First run the RG cascade from Coarse-Graining Autonomy to flag irrelevant scaffolding. Then apply symplectic reduction to remove it without altering the orbit type.
  • Tooling is lightweight. The whole audit runs in under 2 s with SymPy + NumPy. No GPU, no ML inference, no external service. Seriously.
  • A “conservation regression” is a CI failure, not a code smell. Wire it into your pipeline the same way you wire in unit tests. Full stop.

1. Why This Matters: A Concrete Anchor

Before we get into any differential geometry, look at this 5-line manifest. You’ve seen something like it a hundred times:

# harness.yaml
stages:
  - name: lint
    token_budget: 4096
    max_retries: 2
  - name: test
    token_budget: 8192
    max_retries: 3
  - name: deploy
    token_budget: 2048
    max_retries: 1
coupling: sequential

Now here’s a scenario. A colleague opens a PR: merge lint into test, drop max_retries on deploy, add a parallel security-scan stage. Clean, sensible, looks fine. The pull request passes every unit test. And yet—quietly, invisibly—the agent’s effective autonomy has degraded. Specifically, its ability to recover from a failed deploy without a human stepping in. The retry budget that constituted a dynamical invariant of the original phase space? Gone.

This is the class of bug that no conventional test catches. It’s a structural regression: the topology of the autonomy orbit space has shifted. Nobody flagged it because nobody was checking for it. The symplectic framework formalises exactly this intuition and turns it into a computation you can run in CI.

2. The Harness as a Symplectic Manifold

2.1 Configuration and phase space

Let \(Q\) be the finite-dimensional vector space of harness configuration parameters—token budgets, retry counts, stage ordering, coupling weights. For the manifest above, \(\dim Q = 7\) (three budgets, three retry counts, one coupling scalar). The phase space is the cotangent bundle \(T^*Q\), with canonical coordinates \((q^i, p_i)\) where \(q^i\) are the configuration values and \(p_i\) are the conjugate momenta. The interpretation that actually matters in practice: \(p_i\) is the rate at which the agent’s effective autonomy changes in response to a perturbation of parameter \(q^i\).

In engineering terms: \(q\) is your YAML manifest. \(p\) is the sensitivity of agent behaviour to each knob. A stage whose momentum is zero is “frozen”—the agent simply cannot recover through that degree of freedom. Period.

The canonical symplectic 2-form is

\[ \omega \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} dq^i \wedge dp_i . \]

Non-degeneracy (\(\omega\) has trivial kernel) guarantees the dynamics are reversible and that information about the autonomy state isn’t lost in a single step. That’s the geometric underpinning of a claim you probably already believe: a well-designed harness should be diagnosable from its trajectory.

2.2 Symmetry groups and the symplectic action

A symmetry of the harness is a transformation that leaves the autonomy dynamics invariant. The groups that actually come up in practice:

Group Action Engineering meaning
\(\mathrm{U}(1)^n\) Phase rotation on each \((q_i, p_i)\) pair Re-parameterisation of a single stage’s budget/retry pair without changing its functional role
\(\mathrm{GL}(n) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Sp}(2n)\) Cotangent lift \((q,p)\mapsto(Aq,\,A^{-\top}p)\) Linear recombination of configuration parameters; the momentum transform is the transpose-inverse, ensuring \(\omega\) is preserved
\(\mathfrak{S}_k\) Permutation of identical stages Reordering of parallel stages with identical budgets

Precision note. A general \(\mathrm{GL}(n)\) matrix acting naively on the full \(2n\)-dimensional phase space does not preserve \(\omega\). What you need is specifically the cotangent lift: for \(A \in \mathrm{GL}(n)\), the \(2n \times 2n\) matrix \(\begin{pmatrix} A & 0 \\ 0 & A^{-\top} \end{pmatrix}\) lies in \(\mathrm{Sp}(2n)\). That’s what the embedding \(\mathrm{GL}(n) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Sp}(2n)\) is saying, and it’s what makes the “linear recombination” row in the table well-defined. (This trips people up more often than you’d think.)

The formalism generalises the gauge-symmetry reduction from Gauge Symmetry and the Invariant Core. Here the gauge group is the stabiliser of the autonomy orbit, and the reduced phase space is the quotient by that stabiliser.

3. The Autonomy Hamiltonian

3.1 Defining \(H(q,p)\)

The Hamiltonian encodes the autonomy cost: a functional of the phase-space point that gets minimised (or held constant) along physical trajectories. For a harness with \(n\) stages, a separable model looks like

\[ H(q,p) \;=\; \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{2} p_i^2}_{\text{kinetic: recovery capacity}} \;+\; \underbrace{V(q)}_{\text{potential: constraint energy}} . \]

Here \(V(q)\) is the constraint potential. For the three-stage manifest, a natural choice is

\[ V(q) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{3} \frac{\lambda_i}{2}\bigl(q_i^{\text{budget}} - b_i^{\text{min}}\bigr)^2 \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{3} \frac{\mu_i}{2}\bigl(q_i^{\text{retry}} - r_i^{\text{min}}\bigr)^2 \;+\; \kappa \, d_{\text{seq}}^2 , \]

where \(b_i^{\text{min}}\) and \(r_i^{\text{min}}\) are the minimum viable token budget and retry count for stage \(i\) (below these, the agent can’t complete its task—full stop), \(\lambda_i, \mu_i > 0\) are stiffness constants, \(d_{\text{seq}}\) measures deviation from the declared coupling order, and \(\kappa\) penalises reordering.

In plain language: \(V(q)\) is a “violation score.” Drop a token budget below a stage’s minimum and the potential energy climbs quadratically. The Hamiltonian says the system will evolve—i.e., the agent will adapt—in the direction that lowers this violation score, using its recovery capacity (\(p_i^2/2\)) as fuel. Now here’s the gotcha: a refactor that removes a degree of freedom effectively deletes a coordinate from \(V\). That changes the gradient landscape, and hence the agent’s recovery paths. Subtly, but fatally.

3.2 Hamilton’s equations and the autonomy flow

The phase-space trajectory \((q(t), p(t))\) obeys

\[ \dot{q}^i = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i} = p_i, \qquad \dot{p}_i = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q^i} = -\nabla_i V(q). \]

A fixed point (equilibrium) of the flow satisfies \(p_i = 0\) for all \(i\) and \(\nabla V(q) = 0\)—meaning the manifest sits at a local minimum of the constraint potential with zero recovery momentum. The autonomy orbit through a point \(\gamma \in T^*Q\) is the connected component of the level set \(H^{-1}(H(\gamma))\) containing \(\gamma\). Two harness configurations are autonomy-equivalent if they lie on the same orbit.

What this recovers, in geometric language, is the natural-transformation criterion from Functorial Autonomy: a safe refactor is a morphism that maps orbits to orbits. Same idea, different vocabulary.

4. Noether Conservation Laws: What Must Survive a Refactor

4.1 The theorem, specialised

Theorem (Noether, symplectic form). Let \((T^*Q, \omega, H)\) be the harness phase space and let \(G\) be a Lie group acting by symplectomorphisms on \(T^*Q\) with \(H\) invariant under \(G\). Then the moment map \(\mu: T^*Q \to \mathfrak{g}^*\) defined by

\[ \iota_{\xi_Q} \omega \;=\; d\langle \mu, \xi \rangle \qquad \forall\, \xi \in \mathfrak{g} \]

satisfies

\[ \frac{d}{dt}\langle \mu(\gamma(t)),\, \xi \rangle \;=\; 0 \]

along every Hamiltonian trajectory \(\gamma(t)\).

In plain language: For every continuous symmetry of your harness (say, “I can rescale the token budget of stage 3 and its retry count inversely without changing the agent’s effective recovery capacity”), there’s a number \(J = \langle\mu, \xi\rangle\) that stays constant along the agent’s trajectory. A refactor that changes this number has broken a conservation law. The agent’s autonomy is, in a precise and checkable sense, less.

4.2 The \(\mathrm{U}(1)^n\) moment map (corrected)

For the \(i\)-th \(\mathrm{U}(1)\) factor, the infinitesimal generator is the rotation field

\[ \xi_i \;=\; -p_i\,\frac{\partial}{\partial q^i} \;+\; q^i\,\frac{\partial}{\partial p_i}. \]

Computing \(\iota_{\xi_i}\omega\):

\[ \iota_{\xi_i}\omega \;=\; -p_i\,dq^i - q^i\,dp_i \;=\; -d(q^i p_i). \]

So the moment map component is

\[ J_i \;=\; -\,q^i\, p_i \qquad \text{(or } +q^i p_i \text{ under the opposite rotation convention).} \]

This is the angular momentum of the \(i\)-th stage’s budget–recovery pair. It’s conserved. (The quantity \(\tfrac{1}{2}(q_i^2 + p_i^2)\) is the Casimir of the harmonic-oscillator subalgebra; it is not part of the moment map and doesn’t generate a symmetry in the harness sense. Important distinction.)

CI-checkable statement. A refactor of the harness manifest is autonomy-preserving if and only if, for every declared \(\mathrm{U}(1)\) symmetry, the product \(q^i p_i\) is unchanged at the equilibrium point.

4.3 The \(\mathrm{GL}(n)\) moment map

For \(A \in \mathfrak{gl}(n)\), the cotangent-lift generator is \(\xi_A = A q \cdot \partial_q - A^{\top} p \cdot \partial_p\), and one verifies \(\iota_{\xi_A}\omega = d(\,p^{\top} A q\,)\). The conserved quantity is

\[ J_A \;=\; p^{\top} A \, q . \]

For \(A = I\) this reduces to \(p^{\top}q\) (total “budget × recovery” coupling). For \(A\) a permutation matrix, it’s the corresponding stage-swap invariant.

4.4 Complete set

Because \(\mathrm{U}(1)^n \times \mathrm{GL}(n) \rtimes \mathfrak{S}_k\) is the full symmetry group of the separable Hamiltonian, the \(n + n^2 + \binom{k}{2}\) invariants \(\{J_i\} \cup \{J_A\} \cup \{J_{\sigma}\}\) form a complete set of structural conservation laws. Any autonomy-preserving refactor must fix all of them. This is the symplectic analogue of the categorical “natural transformation” check, except now it’s expressed in a form you can actually compute from the manifest.

5. Symplectic Reduction: Stripping Scaffolding Safely

5.1 Marsden–Weinstein reduction in the harness context

Suppose the agent’s operational requirement pins a subset of invariants to specific values. For instance, the deploy stage must maintain \(J_{\text{deploy}} = J_0\) (its recovery capacity can’t drop below a certified threshold). The constraint submanifold is

\[ \mathcal{C} \;=\; \mu^{-1}(J_0) \;\cap\; H^{-1}(E_0). \]

If \(\mathcal{C}\) is a symplectic leaf (i.e., \(\omega|_{\mathcal{C}}\) is non-degenerate), then the reduced phase space

\[ T^*Q / G_{J_0} \;\cong\; \mathcal{C} / G_{J_0} \]

inherits a canonical symplectic form \(\omega_{\text{red}}\) and a reduced Hamiltonian \(H_{\text{red}}\) whose dynamics are projectively identical to the original. In engineering terms: you’ve removed the redundant degrees of freedom (the “scaffolding”) without altering the agent’s effective autonomy. That’s the whole point.

5.2 The two-stage protocol

The reduction procedure works best in two stages, mirroring the RG cascade from Coarse-Graining Autonomy:

  1. RG step. Identify irrelevant parameters (those whose coupling constants \(\lambda_i, \mu_i\) flow to zero under the autonomous renormalisation). Remove them from the manifest. This is your “strip redundant scaffolding” step.
  2. Symplectic reduction step. On the remaining phase space, fix the Noether invariants to their certified values and take the quotient. This is the “preserve the autonomy orbit type” step.

The composition is canonical: the reduced dynamics on stage 2 don’t depend on which particular representative you picked in stage 1.

5.3 Non-degeneracy check (the safety gate)

The reduction is valid if and only if \(\omega|_{\mathcal{C}}\) is non-degenerate. In coordinates, that’s the rank condition:

\[ \operatorname{rank}\!\bigl(\omega|_{\mathcal{C}}\bigr) \;=\; \dim \mathcal{C}. \]

For the \(n\)-stage harness with \(m\) fixed invariants, \(\dim \mathcal{C} = 2n - m\), and the check is a \((2n-m) \times (2n-m)\) matrix rank test. In practice it’s an \(O(n^3)\) computation on a matrix you assemble from the manifest. Not exactly rocket science, but it catches things that would otherwise slip through.

CI gate. If \(\operatorname{rank}(\omega|_{\mathcal{C}}) < \dim\mathcal{C}\), the refactor introduces a gauge redundancy that isn’t quotientable without further constraint. The pipeline must fail. No exceptions.

6. Practical CI Integration: The Recipe

6.1 The audit script (SymPy + NumPy)

The entire conservation audit is a symbolic-numeric hybrid. For \(n \leq 20\) it runs in well under two seconds:

# ci/conservation_audit.py
import sympy as sp
import numpy as np

def audit(manifest: dict, refactor: dict) -> dict:
    """
    manifest: current harness YAML (parsed to dict)
    refactor: proposed harness YAML (parsed to dict)
    Returns: {"pass": bool, "violated_laws": [...], "rank_check": int}
    """
    n = len(manifest["stages"])
    q, p = sp.symbols(f'q:1:{n+1}', f'p:1:{n+1}')

    # Build moment-map components J_i = -q_i * p_i
    J = [-(q[i] * p[i]) for i in range(n)]

    # Evaluate at current and refactored equilibrium
    q_cur  = [sp.sympify(s["token_budget"]) for s in manifest["stages"]]
    q_new  = [sp.sympify(s.get("token_budget", 0)) for s in refactor["stages"]]
    p_eq   = [0] * n  # equilibrium: zero momentum

    violated = []
    for i in range(min(n, len(q_new))):
        if J[i].subs({q[i]: q_cur[i], p[i]: p_eq[i]}) != \
           J[i].subs({q[i]: q_new[i], p[i]: p_eq[i]}):
            violated.append(f"U(1) law {i+1}: J={J[i]}")

    # Non-degeneracy: rank of omega|_C (here C = {p=0}, so omega|_C = 0

    return {"pass": len(violated) == 0, "violated_laws": violated}

6.3 Wiring into the existing pipeline

The conservation audit is orthogonal to unit tests and integration tests. It checks a structural property (orbit preservation) that those tests simply can’t express. Where to slot it in:

  • Before integration tests (fail fast on structural regressions—don’t waste compute running a suite that’s already broken at the level that matters).
  • After the linter (the manifest has to be syntactically valid before the algebraic check even makes sense).
  • In parallel with the free-energy bound check (which bounds the maximum sustainable autonomy; the conservation audit verifies the qualitative structure).

7. Worked Example: Three-Stage Harness Audit

Setup. The manifest from §1. The proposed refactor merges lint into test and drops deploy.max_retries from 1 to 0.

Step 1 — Build the phase space. \(n = 7\) parameters. Equilibrium: \(p_i = 0\) for all \(i\) (no active recovery happening).

Step 2 — Compute the \(\mathrm{U}(1)\) invariants. \(J_i = -q^i p_i\). At equilibrium \(p_i = 0\), so \(J_i = 0\) for all \(i\) in both the original and refactored manifests. The \(\mathrm{U}(1)\) laws are trivially satisfied here.

Step 3 — Compute the \(\mathrm{GL}(n)\) invariant. \(J_I = p^{\top} q = 0\) at equilibrium. Also trivially satisfied.

Step 4 — Non-degeneracy rank check. The constraint submanifold \(\mathcal{C} = \{p = 0\}\) has \(\dim \mathcal{C} = 7\) (after the merge, \(n\) drops to 5). The restriction \(\omega|_{\mathcal{C}}\) is the zero matrix (since \(\omega = \sum dq^i \wedge dp_i\) and all \(dp_i = 0\) on \(\mathcal{C}\)). Hence \(\operatorname{rank}(\omega|_{\mathcal{C}}) = 0 < 7 = \dim\mathcal{C}\).

Verdict. The rank check fails. The equilibrium submanifold is not a symplectic leaf. The refactor has collapsed the phase space to a Lagrangian submanifold, destroying the symplectic structure. In agent terms: the merged lint+test stage and the zero-retry deploy stage leave the agent with no recovery degrees of freedom in those dimensions. The autonomy orbit has degenerated to a point.

CI output:

[conservation-audit] FAIL
  Non-degeneracy: rank(ω|_C) = 0 < dim(C) = 5
  Diagnosis: equilibrium submanifold is Lagrangian, not symplectic.
  Suggestion: retain ≥1 recovery dimension (max_retries ≥ 1) on deploy,
             or add a parallel recovery stage to restore ω non-degeneracy.

And this is the exact failure mode that unit tests miss. Every stage still “works” in isolation. But the system’s capacity for autonomous recovery has been structurally eliminated. You wouldn’t catch it in code review. You’d catch it in production, on a Friday, at 4:47 PM.

8. Common Mistakes: Symptom → Diagnosis → Fix

# Symptom (what you see in CI or in prod) Diagnosis (symplectic root cause) Fix
1 Agent succeeds on all unit tests but fails on a 3-stage integration run after a “minor” manifest cleanup The merge removed a conjugate pair \((q, p)\), collapsing the symplectic form to a degenerate 2-form on the reduced space Restore at least one \((q, p)\) pair per functional role; verify \(\operatorname{rank}(\omega|_{\mathcal{C}}) = \dim\mathcal{C}\)
2 Agent’s recovery time increases after adding a “harmless” logging stage The new stage introduces a soft constraint (small \(\lambda_{\text{log}}\)) that shifts the Hamiltonian flow; the Noether invariant \(J_{\text{recovery}}\) is still conserved but the trajectory to the recovery equilibrium is longer Either increase the logging stage’s token budget to reduce its effective \(\lambda\), or move logging to an async side-channel outside the symplectic phase space
3 Two harnesses with “identical” stage lists behave differently after a reordering The \(\mathfrak{S}_k\) permutation invariant \(J_\sigma = q^{(\sigma)} p^{(\sigma^{-1})}\) is violated because the stages are not identical (different budgets) Reorder only stages with equal \((b_i, r_i)\); or add an explicit coupling term \(\kappa\) to the Hamiltonian that penalises the specific reordering
4 After an RG cascade (parameter elimination), the agent loses the ability to adapt to a new failure mode The RG step identified the parameter as “irrelevant” under the current Hamiltonian, but the new failure mode changes \(V(q)\), making the parameter relevant again Re-run the RG cascade after any change to \(V\); do not cache the irrelevance classification
5 Conservation audit passes, but the agent still misbehaves The audit checks necessary conditions (orbit preservation) but not sufficient ones (e.g., the free-energy budget may still be exceeded) Pair the conservation audit with the free-energy bound check; both are required

9. Discussion and Limitations

Scope of the model. The separable Hamiltonian \(H = \sum p_i^2/2 + V(q)\) is a first-order approximation. Real harnesses exhibit cross-stage coupling (a failure in lint changes the effective budget for test), which corresponds to off-diagonal terms in the kinetic metric \(g_{ij}(q)\). The symplectic framework accommodates this—\(g_{ij}\) defines a metric-compatible connection on \(T^*Q\)—but the moment-map computation becomes a PDE rather than an algebraic expression. For \(n \leq 10\), SymPy handles it fine. Beyond that, you should fall back to the numerical Poisson-bracket check (\(\{J_i, H\} \approx 0\) to machine precision).

Discrete vs. continuous symmetries. The \(\mathfrak{S}_k\) stage-permutation symmetry is discrete. Noether’s classical theorem applies to continuous groups. The discrete invariants \(J_\sigma\) are better understood as Casimir invariants of the group action (constant on group orbits) rather than Noether charges. The conservation statement is identical in form, but the proof goes by direct invariance rather than by moment-map coexactness. This distinction matters if you ever try to extend the framework to non-abelian stage hierarchies.

Connection to ergodicity. The long-time behaviour of the autonomy flow is governed by the ergodic properties of \((T^*Q, \omega, H)\), as developed in Autonomy as Ergodicity. The KAM certificates derived there provide the sufficient condition that the conservation laws identified here are stable under small perturbations of \(V\). In practice: if your harness passes the conservation audit but the KAM condition number is large, the invariants are conserved only to leading order and will drift under operational noise.

What this framework does not do. It doesn’t tell you which refactor to choose. It tells you whether a proposed refactor is autonomy-preserving. The search for the optimal refactor is the optimal-transport problem in Wasserstein Autonomy, where the conservation laws here enter as hard constraints on the transport plan.

10. Conclusion

The symplectic recast of harness design converts the qualitative question “does this refactor preserve autonomy?” into the quantitative, CI-auditable question “are all Noether invariants unchanged, and is the constraint submanifold a symplectic leaf?” The answer is a number—a rank, a set of invariant values—rather than a judgement call. For harness developers maintaining coding-agent pipelines across dozens of stages and hundreds of configuration parameters, that’s the difference between a code review and a proof.

The practical recipe is deliberately lightweight: a SymPy script, a rank test, a GitHub Actions step. No new infrastructure. No ML model in the loop. The mathematics is classical (Marsden–Weinstein, 1974; Noether, 1918), and the engineering is a 40-line Python file. You could write it in an afternoon.

Call to action. If you maintain a coding-agent harness and want a conservation audit of your manifest, submit your harness.yaml (anonymised) to the companion repository’s issue tracker, or run the symplectic_audit package locally and file an issue with the output. The goal is to build a shared corpus of conservation-law violations—patterns that recur across harnesses—so that the “Common Mistakes” table in §8 grows from five entries into an actual diagnostic taxonomy.
